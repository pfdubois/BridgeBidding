40. Summary Tables¶

This chapter collects the summary charts for the chapters on responding to openings.

Summary of 1N Responses¶

Here is a summary of the responses to a 1N Opening, classified by hand type, and hand strength.

Balanced or Semi-Balanced Hands¶

This chart is for the standard 15-17 HCP 1N opener and a balanced responding hand (4-3-3-3, 4-4-3-2, or 5-3-3-2).

Unbalanced Hands¶

One 5+ Card Major M (no 4-card other major)¶

Weak (0-7 HCP): Jacoby transfer (2♦ → hearts, 2♥ → spades) then pass.

Invitational (8-9 HCP):

  • With a 5-card major, transfer, then bid 2NT after opener accepts.

  • With 6+, transfer, then bid 3M.

Game Force (10-15 HCP):

  • With a 5-card major, transfer, then bid 3NT.

  • With 6+, transfer, then bid 4M. See Texas Transfers.

Slam Interest (|16ormore| HCP):

  • With a 5-card major, transfer, then bid 3NT.

  • With 6+, transfer then quantitative 4M.

** Super-Accepts (Advanced)**:

  • Opener may jump to 3M with a maximum (17 HCP) and good trump support (See Advanced Notrump for variations).

Follow-ups after Opener Accepts the Transfer

After 1N – 2t – 2M:

  • New minor at 3-level = game force with a second suit. Could be strong.

  • 3M = invitational with 6+ trumps.

  • 3NT = to play (opener can correct to 4M with a fit).

  • 4♣ = Gerber.

  • 4M = 6+ trumps. See Texas Transfers, recommended. If playing Texas, this sequence is a mild slam try (“slow shows”).

  • 4NT = quantitative.

One Four-Card Major¶

Use Stayman but NOT if your hand is 4=3=3=3 or 3=4=3=3. The hand should be invitational or better, unless weak, short in clubs, intending to pass opener’s rebid regardless.

Bid 2♣︎; the opener rebids a 4+-card major, or bids 2♦︎ to deny one. If opener rebids your major, revalue your hand, then:

  • Raise to 3M with an invitational hand.

  • Raise to 4M with 10+ points.

  • If you have slam interest (16+) you can power-raise by bidding 3 of the other major (3W!), which invites opener to start bidding a control.

If opener doesn’t show a fit then bid NT at an appropriate level as in the no-major case.

Both Majors (4-4, 5-4)¶

If weak (0-7 HCP), transfer to a five-card major and pass, unless Garbage Stayman applies (4=4=4=1 or 4=4=5=0).

Otherwise start with Stayman 2♣ (artificial, asks for a 4-card major). Opener replies:

  • 2♦ = no 4-card major

  • 2♥ = 4+ hearts (may have spades)

  • 2♠ = 4+ spades (denies 4 hearts)

Then invite or raise to game in a discovered fit, or bid 2NT/3NT if no fit.

Optional: With a game-forcing hand, after a 2♦ denial, use Smolen.

5-5 Majors¶

You may have an agreement at the 3-level showing these hands. If not, which is the default:

  • Weak: Transfer to the better major then pass

  • Invitational: Transfer to hearts then bid 2♠.

  • Game forcing: Transfer to spades then bid 3♥︎.

Quiz: How do we know the responder is 5-5 in the majors in these cases?

Answer: he did not bid Stayman.

Long Minor (no 4-card major)¶

Weak (0-7 HCP only): 2♠ (Minor Relay):

  • Forces opener to bid 3♣. Responder then bids pass or corrects to 3♦ (to play).

Invitational (8-9 HCP):

  • Direct 3♣ or 3♦ (natural invite). If you are playing the 3-level bids as something else, you’ll have another way.

Game Force (10-15 HCP):

  • Minor Relay, then bid 3NT.

Slam Interest (|16ormore| HCP):

  • Bid 2♠︎ (Minor Relay), then bid 3♥︎ to show interest in a club slam, or 3♠︎ to show interest in a diamond slam. This is part of the Minor Relay convention.

Quick Reference¶

These are the most common standard responses:

  • Pass: weak

  • 2♣: Stayman (4+ major(s) or special weak hands)

  • 2♦: Jacoby transfer to hearts (5+ hearts)

  • 2♥: Jacoby transfer to spades (5+ spades)

  • 2♠: Minor Relay (6+ minor, usually weak)

  • 2NT: balanced, invitational (8-9)

  • 3NT: balanced game (10-15)

  • 4NT: quantitative slam try

  • 4♦/4♥: Texas transfer (optional, game force with 6+ major)

After An Overcall: 1M (overcall)¶

Responder’s action

Meaning

Pass

Weak hands. Also pass with a trump stack in their suit, and let opener reopen.

Simple raise

2M, 3+ trump, 6-10–; Axxx of trump and a ruffing value is also enough.

Limit raise, 3-card

Cue bid of their suit: limit raise or better.

Limit raise, 4-card

The same cue bid. By partnership agreement, 2N when the cue bid would force to game, as in 1♥ (2♠).

Game-forcing raise

Cue bid, then game. 1♥ (2♠) 3♠ is game forcing.

Splinter

A jump cue bid.

Preemptive raise

A jump raise to 3M is preemptive, usually four trump. 4M: weak, 5+ trump.

1N

1♥ (1♠) 1N only: typically shows a spade stopper but not enough values for 2N, say 8-9 HCP.

New suit at the 1 level

Not applicable.

New suit at the 2 level

10+ points and a five-card suit. Forcing, but not game forcing.

Jump shift

Weak (preemptive).

2N

Natural: invitational, balanced, a stopper in their suit. Can be passed.

3N

To play, with stoppers in their suit.

Double

Negative double (through 2♠): 6 points at the one level, 8 to 10 at the two level.

Redouble

Not applicable.

Cue bid of their suit

Limit raise or better in opener’s major. A jump cue bid is a splinter.

Two-Way Checkback¶

This summarizes Two-Way New Minor Forcing, also called Two-Way Checkback Stayman. It applies after 1m – 1M – 1N. It is off by a passed hand and off if the opponents overcall a suit, but on over a takeout double.

Responder’s Rebid After 1m – 1M – 1N¶

Responder’s rebid

Meaning

2♣!

Relay; opener must bid 2♦!. Invitational with five of M or four of the other major, or a weak hand with diamonds (pass 2♦).

2♦!

Game forcing; opener describes his hand (see below).

2M

To play, with 5+ cards in M.

2♠ after 1m – 1♥ – 1N

4-4 in the majors, invitational values.

2♥ after 1m – 1♠ – 1N

Five spades and four hearts, minimal; opener passes or corrects to 2♠.

2N

Natural, balanced invitation, with no interest in a major fit.

3♣

Weak, to play.

3♦

Slam try in diamonds.

3♥ after 1m – 1♠ – 1N

Slam try, 5-5 in the majors.

3M

Slam try, with a good suit.
Responder’s Next Bid After 1m – 1M – 1N – 2♣! – 2♦!¶

Responder’s bid

Meaning

Pass

Signoff in 2♦.

2 of the other major

Invitational with five of M and four of the other major (2♥ after 1♠; 2♠ after 1♥).

2M

Mildly invitational with 5+ cards in M (a weak six-card suit too). Opener passes with a minimum, raises to three with a fit and a medium hand or to four with a fit and a maximum, and bids 2N with a maximum and no fit.

2N

Balanced invitation, denying five of M. Opener bids 3N with a maximum, passes with a minimum.

3♣ or 3♦

Invitational with four of M and that minor (support for opener’s minor, such as four cards, or a decent suit).

3♥ after 1♠

Invitational, 5-5 in the majors.

3M

Invitational with six good cards in M.
Opener’s Reply After 1m – 1M – 1N – 2♦!¶

Opener’s bid

Meaning

2 of the other major

Four cards in the other major (shown first).

2M

Three-card support for M. With a flat hand and the points mostly outside M, consider 2N.

3m

Neither of the above; a good suit of 5+ cards and a good hand.

2N

The default bid: nothing more to show.

Jumps

Opener should not jump (not to 3N either); the auction is already game forcing.

The same structure also works after 1♥ – 1♠ – 1N, since a responder with hearts would have raised rather than bid 1♠.

Responding To Partner’s Overcall¶

This summarizes Responding To Partner’s Overcall. Partner’s overcall shows 8-17 points.

Advancer’s Bids After (1x) Overcall (Pass)¶

Advancer’s action

Meaning

Pass

Nothing to say. The overcall is not forcing.

New suit

Natural and constructive, but not forcing (unpassed hand). Usually denies a fit.

Simple raise

Competitive: 6-10 points, 3+ trump.

Cue bid of their suit

The one forcing bid. Usually a limit raise or better with 3+ trump; can be any strong hand, clarified next round.

Jump raise

Preemptive: weak, usually four trump.

Jump cue bid

By agreement only: a mixed raise, four trump and about 7-10 points.

1N

About 8-11 points, a stopper in their suit.

2N

Invitational, about 11-12 points, a stopper in their suit.

After a 1N overcall

Systems on: respond as to a 1N opening.
