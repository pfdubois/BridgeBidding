Advanced Checkback
==================

.. index::
   single: checkback; advanced
   see: Two-Way Checkback Stayman; Two-Way New Minor Forcing
   see: TWNMF; Two-Way New Minor Forcing
   
The Checkback Family is New Minor Forcing (NMF) and Fourth Suit Forcing (4sf),
described in the chapter, :ref:`Finding 5-3 and 4-4 Major Fits <BasicCheckback>`.  
NMF has two variants, Two-Way New Minor Forcing (TWNMF) and XYZ, described in this 
chapter. We also describe advanced schemes when opener's rebid is 2N.

:ref:`Two-way New Minor Forcing (TWNMF) <TWNMF>`, also called Two-Way
Checkback Stayman, distinguishes invitational vs. game-forcing hands by
using both 2♣ and 2♦ as artificial and is universally used among experts
instead of NMF.

:ref:`XYZ <XYZ>` extends the idea of TWNMF to the case of the opener's rebid being 1♠, and
is so-named because the auction has begun 1x – 1y – 1z. Since it is identical to 
TWNMF if "z" is no-trump, the two conventions are played together. In this approach, it is 
still possible to have a fourth-suit auction such as 1♥ – 1♠ – 2♣ – 2♦!. 

In case the opener's rebid is a jump to 2NT, showing an 18-19 balanced hand, we 
still have NMF. The :ref:`Wolff Signoff <Wolff>` is a popular variation. 
We present with it :ref:`an idea of Marc Smith extending TWNMF to this case <NMF2>`.

.. _TWNMF:

Two-Way New Minor Forcing
-------------------------

Two-way New Minor Forcing (TWNMF), also called Two-Way Checkback Stayman, applies after 
a 1N rebid by opener after responder bids a major. Responder distinguishes invitational 
vs. game-forcing hands with his first rebid.

Playing this convention instead of NMF is, as Marty Bergen says, "Infinitely easier 
and better".

.. note::

   Some also play TWNMF after auctions that begin 1♣ – 1♦; I do not, to avoid
   confusion with the Walsh style. Likewise, some do not play it after 1♥ – 1♠ – 1N.

TWNMF is off by a passed hand. It is off if the opponents overcall a suit, but on
over a takeout double.

Too Weak To Checkback
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

With a balanced 11 HCP or less, just pass. Yes, on rare occasion an 11 opposite 14
might just make a game, but you're hunting for the perfect hand and may get too high.
In the long run passing is wiser.

With a five-card major, just rebid it. This is a drop-dead bid since you have not 
done one of the checkback bids below. If you think about it, the 1N rebid is a "weak" 
notrump bid. If you were playing weak notrumps, you would transfer to the major and 
pass. This is the same thing. 


Checkback: Invitational Hands
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. rubric:Four-Four In The Majors  

With an invitational hand that is 4-4 exactly in the majors, we use the sequence:

* 1m – 1♥︎ – 1N - 2♠︎

Opener must recognize this isn't some sort of reverse. Opener continues naturally.

:rubric:The 2♣︎ Checkback

We bid 2♣! (artificial) with invitational hands containing five of our 
major or four of the other major. Opener must rebid 2♦! (forced). 
The 2♣︎ bid can be explained as "invitational with five of our major or four of the
other major, or a weak hand with diamonds".  

After 1m – 1M – 1N, a weak hand with five cards in M should rebid 2M, to play.

Then, in the case of 1m – 1♠ – 1N – 2♣! (artificial) – 2♦! (forced), responder bids:

* Pass – signoff in 2♦.
* 2♥ – Invitational with 5 spades and 4 hearts.
* 2♠ – Mildly invitational with |5ormore| spades.
* 2NT – A balanced invitation, denying five spades. Opener bids 3N with a maximum
  and passes with a minimum.
* 3♣ – Invitational with 4 spades and clubs.
* 3♦ – Invitational with 4 spades and diamonds.
* 3♥ – Invitational with 5-5 in the majors.
* 3♠ – Invitational with 6 good spades. With a weak six-card suit, rebid 2♠ instead.

If responder's suit had been hearts:

* Pass – signoff in 2♦.
* 2♥ – Mildly invitational with |5ormore| hearts
* 2♠ – Invitational with 5 hearts and four spades
* 2NT – A balanced invitation, denying five hearts.
* 3♣ – Invitational with 4 hearts and clubs
* 3♦ – Invitational with 4 hearts and diamonds
* 3♥ – Invitational with 6 good hearts; with a weak six-card suit, rebid 2♥ instead.

When responder rebids 2M, mildly invitational, opener:

* Passes with a minimum, with or without a fit.
* Raises to three with a fit and a medium hand.
* Raises to four with a fit and a maximum.
* Bids 2N with a maximum and no fit.

A responder with a balanced invitation and no interest in pursuing a major fit may
instead raise 1N to 2N directly, a natural invitation, without using TWNMF.
In the sequences above, the 3m bids show either support for the 1m opener (for example,
four-card support for opener's minor) or a decent suit, and invitational values.

Checkback: Game-Forcing Hands
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

We bid 2♦! (artificial) with any game-forcing hand; the auction is then forcing
to game. The responses for 2♦! are easier; we are not
stopping short of game and use a slow-shows approach to slam. Opener should not
jump. Priorities for opener remain as in NMF:

* With four of the other major, bid it: 2♥ after 1♠, 2♠ after 1♥.
* With 3-card support for M, opener should bid 2M. However, with a flat hand and
  the points mostly not in M, consider 2N.
* With neither, rebid three of your minor with a good suit of five or more cards
  and a good hand.
* Otherwise bid 2N, the default bid when there is nothing more to show. Do not
  jump to 3N; leave room for responder to keep describing his hand.
  
Two-Suited Minimal Hands
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

If you have a minimal hand and bypassed a five-card diamond suit to bid a four-card
major, you can bid the 2♣! (relay) and pass the obligatory 2♦! (forced).

With 5 or more in M, bid 2M to drop dead.  With five spades and four hearts,
bid 2♥ showing your shape; opener can pass or correct.  It should do better than 1N.

Finally, we have the problem of a weak hand with clubs.  A bid of 3♣ after the 1N 
rebid is minimal and to play.  (With a better hand we'd go through the 2♣ or 2♦ bids 
first and then bid 3♣).

Direct Three-Level Bids
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

After the 1N rebid, a direct bid of 3M is a slam try with a good suit.  We could 
go through 2♦! first if we were just game-forcing. A jump to 3♦, such as 
1♦ – 1♥ – 1N – 3♦, is a slam try in diamonds, and after 1m – 1♠ – 1N a jump to 3♥
is a slam try with 5-5 in the majors. But a jump to 3♣ is weak, as noted above.

Optionally, you can make 3♣ a slam try too and show the weak hand in clubs by bidding::
    
    1N  2N! (relay to 3♣)
    3♣! (forced)  Pass
    
This is possible since a plain balanced invite begins with the 2♣ relay so the direct
2N is an unused bid. The problem is that the 2N bid may be error-prone. So I prefer
having 1m – 1M – 1N – 2N just be natural too and lose the 3♣ slam-try bid.

The summary table for these responses is in
:ref:`summary_checkback` in the Summary Charts chapter.

Gavin Wolpert has a great advanced video lesson on this topic, "Two-Way
New Minor Forcing". He has additional ideas.

XYZ
---

.. index::
   pair: convention; XYZ
   single: checkback; XYZ
        
XYZ is a convention that applies when the first three bids are on the one level, 
and responder has an invitational or better hand. The name comes from writing this as
1X – 1Y – 1Z.

Essentially we extend the ideas of TWNMF to these additional auctions.
 
Research XYZ with a partner after having plenty of TWNMF experience.

Wolff Signoff 
-------------

In the section on :ref:`New Minor Forcing <NMF>`, we saw that in auctions where 
the opener rebids 2N to show an 18-19 balanced hand, 1m – 1M – 2N, that a bid of 3w!,
the other minor, is an artificial bid asking if opener has three-card support of M.
The Wolff Signoff, akin to :ref:`TWNMF <TWNMF>` disregards which minor was opened
and uses 3♣︎ to show weak hands:

* 3♣! is a mandatory relay to 3♦; see below for examples. This is a 
  :ref:`Lebensohl-like <Lebensohl>` relay to show various weak hands. 
* 3♦! is the Stayman-like ("Staymanic") game-forcing bid.
* 3M shows |5ormore| cards.

There are various ideas needing partnership agreement for other bids.

The 3♣ puppet to 3♦ is used for a number of weak hand types:

* After 1♣ – 1♠ – 2N – holding a hand like Jxxxx/xx/QJxxx/x intending to pass
  partner's forced 3♦.

* After 1♥ – 1♠ – 2NT – Jxxx/x/KJxxxx/xx same

* After 1x – 1♠ – 2NT – Q109xxx/x/Qxx/xxx intending to sign off in 3♠

* After 1m – 1♠ – 2N – Qxxxx/Kxxxx/xx/x intending to bid 3♥ to offer a choice of
  3♥/3♠
  
* After 1♦ – 1♠ – 2N – Jxxx/x/xx/KJxxxx responder bids 3N over 3♦ showing weak with
  long clubs – opener can pass or convert to 4♣


The 3♦ is looking for major fits:

* After 1♣ – 1♦ – 2N – 3♦ holding something like Kxxx/Qxxx/Axxx/x perhaps – might also have
  only one major.

  If opener bids 3♥ and responder continues with 3♠, that says I wasn't
  interested in your hearts but I have four spades.
  This sequence probably has slam interest as 3NT would also suggest four
  spades but no interest beyond game.

* After 1m – 1♥ – 2N – 3♦ – responder may have any hand with five hearts, just looking
  to find a 5-3 fit.
  
  Responder might also have 4-4 in majors. If opener shows 3♥, he can
  continue with 3♠/3N as above.

* 1m – 1♠ – 2N – 3♦ – responder could have any hand looking for a 5-3 spade fit,
  or 5-4 in majors looking for 4-4 hearts or 5-3 spades.

Slam Tries
~~~~~~~~~~

Marc Smith suggests this addition to the Wolff Signoff.

* 3♥! is a slam try in opener's suit. It sets m as trump.
* 3♠! is a slam try in responder's suit. It sets M as trump.


After the 1x – 1M – 2N – 3♥/♠ slam tries, responder has set trumps and opener should 
make control bids.
